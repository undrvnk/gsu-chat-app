# GSU CS Graduate Admissions Q&A

These are official-style answers used by the GSU CS graduate program. Use them
to answer similar admissions questions.

## General inquiries on assistantship
You are welcome to apply. Limited assistantships are automatically considered by
the admission committee, especially for highly achieved applicants. Admitted
students also have opportunities to apply for other assistantships once they get
on campus. After arriving at the department and checking in, you will join a
mailing list where GRA/GTA opportunities are posted.

## Assistantship inquiry from admitted MS students
Congratulations on your admission. Admitted students have limited opportunities
to apply for some assistantships once they get to campus. After arriving and
checking into the department, you will join a mailing list where GRA/GTA
opportunities are posted.

## Assistantship inquiry from admitted PhD students without support
Congratulations on your admission. For PhD students, full support comes from the
faculty/advisor. You are welcome to contact faculty to secure such support. In
addition, admitted students have limited opportunities to apply for some limited
assistantships once they get to campus and join the department mailing list.

## Application fee waiver request
The Graduate School provides application fee waivers for applicants who have
participated in programs designed to diversify graduate programs. Eligibility
must be verified; access specifics from the Application Fee Waivers tab within
the graduate application (https://graduate.gsu.edu/how-to-apply/#app-fee-waivers).
Waivers are not offered for individual circumstances or hardships. The CS
department does not handle application fees; contact the Office of Graduate
Admissions for questions about fee payment or document submission.

Qualifying programs include:
- Honorably Discharged U.S. Veterans, Active Duty Military, Guard or Reserves
- Maximizing Access to Research Careers (MARC) U-STAR Program
- Initiative for Maximizing Student Development (IMSD)
- Louis Stokes Alliances for Minority Participation (LSAMP)
- McNair Scholars
- Bridges to the Doctorate
- Annual Biomedical Research Conference for Minority Students (ABRCMS) attendance
- Gates Millennium Scholars (GMS) Program
- Research Initiative for Scientific Advancement (RISE)
- Post-Baccalaureate Research Education Program (PREP)
- SACNAS Conference Attendance
- Completion of GSU's Center for the Advancement of Students and Alumni's 25 to Thrive Program
- Teach for America Participants
- Returned Peace Corps Volunteers

## Defer request
You can request to defer admission up to one year. Include a rationale and the
term you would like to defer to. Assistantships (if any) cannot be deferred. A
deferral request must be approved prior to the start of the semester.

## English proficiency
GSU recommends a minimum IELTS score of 6.5 (USG minimum 6) and a TOEFL score
above 80.

## TOEFL waiver request
TOEFL/IELTS is not required of international applicants who received a degree
from a regionally accredited U.S. institution, or from outside the U.S. where
English is the official language of academic instruction, or who were born in an
English-speaking country. Proof of a U.S. degree must accompany the application.
Exempt countries include: Antigua and Barbuda, Australia, The Bahamas, Belize,
Barbados, Bermuda, British Virgin Islands, Canada, Cayman Islands, Christmas
Island, Cook Islands, Dominica, Falkland Islands, The Gambia, Ghana, Gibraltar,
Grenada, Guam, Guyana, Ireland, Jamaica, Jersey, Malawi, Micronesia, Montserrat,
New Zealand, Nigeria, Pitcairn Islands, Saint Kitts and Nevis, Saint Lucia,
Saint Martin, Saint Vincent/Grenadines, South Africa, St. Helena, Trinidad and
Tobago, Turks and Caicos Islands, Uganda, United Kingdom/Great Britain, United
States of America, Virgin Islands.

## GRE waiver request
Currently, the GRE is not required but is accepted for all applicants. To request
a GRE waiver, provide evidence of academic excellence and English proficiency as
a SINGLE PDF file with a brief statement of achievements (publications, research
experience, transcripts, high GPA, work experience, etc.). Admission is
competition-based; only a limited number of top candidates can be admitted.

## Possibility to get admitted
Submit your application through the college admission portal
(https://cas.gsu.edu/graduate-admissions/). The graduate admission committee will
evaluate your application. GRE is expected above 35% for Verbal and Quantitative;
TOEFL above 80. English proficiency is not the only evaluation metric. Only top
candidates can be admitted due to limited resources.

## Non-CS majors applying to the CS MS program
The CS department welcomes students from all backgrounds, but some foundation
courses are required (https://catalogs.gsu.edu/preview_program.php?catoid=26&poid=7407).
Non-CS majors are recommended to complete CSC 1000-3000 level courses with a
grade of 'B' or better before applying, or consider GSU's post-baccalaureate
program (https://admissions.gsu.edu/bachelors-degree/apply/other/#postbaccalaureate).
Missed courses can be taken at GSU. The department also admits non-degree
students (https://cas.gsu.edu/graduate-services/non-degree-transient-admission/).

Sample post-bac schedule:
- Fall: CSC 1301
- Spring: CSC 1302 and CSC 2510
- Summer: CSC 2720, CSC 3210, CSC 3320
- Then apply for the next Fall MS in CS intake. While enrolled, make up missed
  4000/6000 foundation courses (6210, 6320, 6330/6340/6510, 6350, 6520).

## Foundation courses
The following CS foundation courses are expected, each with a grade of B or
higher: Calculus; Discrete Math or Theoretical Foundations of CS; Data
Structures; Design and Analysis of Algorithms; Computer Architecture or Computer
Organization; Operating Systems; Automata or Programming Language Concepts or
Compilers; Software Engineering. If not all are taken, talented admitted students
may be asked to complete missing foundation courses in the first semester before
starting MS coursework. GRE (and TOEFL for international students) is also
required. MS catalog: https://catalog.gsu.edu/graduate20202021/college-of-arts-and-sciences/#computer-science

## Application status inquiry
Once an application is complete it is ready for review. The committee conducts a
holistic review over several weeks to months based on goals, work/research
experience, essays, recommendation letters, academic records, and more. You will
be notified by email when your application status changes in the portal. Please
be patient.

## Attracting strong candidates / funding facts
In the recent 5 years, more than 95% of graduate students were funded
(fellowships, teaching assistantships, research assistantships, on-campus jobs).
Typically graduate students receive a full tuition waiver ($35,000-$50,000
savings) and a ~$2,000 stipend per semester (up to 2 semesters, depending on
budget and performance). MS program details:
https://www.cs.gsu.edu/graduate/master-of-science/

## Application status inquiry from recommended PhD students
If you do not request financial support, you are qualified to be admitted.
Otherwise, you are added to the candidate pool to see if a faculty member would
like to recruit you and offer a GTA/GRA assistantship.

## Assistantship / financial aid inquiries
Applicants are expected to secure a faculty advisor prior to admission,
particularly for funding. To get funding, find a PhD advisor who can support you
from grants or petition the department for a GTA scholarship. All accepted
students are considered for graduate assistantship at the time of admission;
some top students are admitted with funding. Even without initial funding, you
can apply for funding within the department once enrolled. For visa, employment,
and scholarship questions for international students, contact International
Student and Scholar Services (ISSS): https://isss.gsu.edu/about/contact/.
Around 90% of MS students were funded in previous years.

Types of support:
- Graduate Teaching Assistantship (GTA): a limited number offered by the
  department each semester, contingent on teaching needs and funds.
- Graduate Research Assistantship (GRA): granted only by individual faculty
  members, not the College or department. Review faculty research interests
  (https://csds.gsu.edu/directory/) and reach out only to aligned professors with
  a clear outline of your potential contributions. Professors do not offer GTAs.

Newly admitted next steps: https://cas.gsu.edu/graduate-admissions/newly-admitted-next-steps/

## Graduate assistantship and tuition waiver in the admission letter
Support is guaranteed for the first semester and can be renewed in following
semesters depending on academic standing (GPA), GTA work performance, and
department need. Most MS students work as GTAs/GRAs or find on-campus jobs and
have tuition waived.

## Spring PhD applications
PhD applications are usually not accepted for Spring. However, if a professor
wants to recruit you, the case can be specially handled. Contact professors to
see if any are interested. GRE Verbal and Quantitative scores are expected to be
at least 35%.

## Inquiry from rejected students
Feedback cannot be provided to individual applicants due to the large number of
applications. The program is increasingly competitive and only a limited number
of top candidates can be admitted.

## Re-evaluation request
Applications cannot be re-evaluated. The committee carefully considers each
application using multiple criteria (GRE, TOEFL, GPA, experience, foundation
courses, statement of purpose, recommendation letters).

## Visa
The department does not handle visa issues. All immigration issues are handled by
International Student & Scholar Services (ISSS): https://isss.gsu.edu/. F-1 visa
I-20 process: https://isss.gsu.edu/future-students/admissions/i-20-process-for-f-1-students/

### Enrollment requirements for F-1 visa holders
You must maintain full-time enrollment (at least 12 credit hours each semester).
Of those, a minimum of 9 credit hours must be on campus (in-person or hybrid);
up to 3 credit hours may be online. Failure to meet these may violate F-1 status.

## Admission criteria
All applications are evaluated by the Graduate Admission Committee based on GPA,
prior experience, statement of purpose, and recommendation letters. GRE is
expected above 35% for Verbal and Quantitative; TOEFL above 80. A GRE waiver may
be requested with evidence of academic excellence and English proficiency.

## Change from College of Business
The CS program belongs to the College of Arts and Sciences (CAS), not the College
of Business. To be admitted to CAS, apply for admission there; requirements are
on the CAS graduate program website.

## Deferring with I-20 update process
Once an admission deferral is approved, you will receive instructions on how to
request a deferred I-20 for the next available term, needed for the visa
interview and travel. For help, use QLess (Georgia State's virtual advising
queue): join remotely, schedule virtual/in-person meetings, with international
call-backs available. Advising hours (EST): Mon/Wed/Fri 1:00-4:00 PM, Tue
9:00 AM-12:00 PM, no advising Thursdays. Queue: https://isss.gsu.edu/advising/

## Transcript hold
Per University System of Georgia (USG) policy, all admitted students must have
official transcripts (and degree certificates if applicable) sent directly from
each previous institution, including community colleges and institutions where
transfer credit was earned, regardless of where the final degree was conferred.
If unable to obtain official documents, complete the Transcript Hold Extension
Request form with an explanation. Questions: submit a Help Ticket.
